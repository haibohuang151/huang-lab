Research

Research Area 1: Process Development to Convert Agricultural Products and Food Waste into Value-added Products

Food production and distribution results in a large quantity of food waste and processing byproducts, which usually contain valuable compounds such as dietary fibers and proteins. Valorization of these wastes and byproducts to produce food ingredients and chemicals can improve the sustainability and profitability of the food industry.

Our lab is working on the following areas: 1) produce plant-based protein from food processing byproducts, 2) separate and modify dietary fibers from agricultural byproducts, and 3) convert agricultural biomass into functional materials, such as hard carbons, biochemicals, and adsorbents.

Currently Active Projects:
  • Plant-based protein production from brewer's spent grain and their potential applications
  • Extraction and modification of highly fermentable dietary fiber production from food processing byproducts
  • Conversion of agricultural biomass into porous carbon as energy storage materials

Research Area 2: Fermentation Technologies to Produce Food Ingredients and Biochemicals

Microbial fermentation has the potential to produce a wide array of alcohols, food ingredients, and renewable chemicals. This potential can be enhanced through novel bioreactor design, cost-effective feedstocks, and high-yield microorganisms. Despite its long history, fermentation still faces challenges, including slow conversion rates, low product titer, impure products in fermentation broth, and costly downstream purification — all of which highlight the need for innovation in fermentation technology.

Our lab focuses on: 1) designing customized reactors to improve fermentation efficiency; 2) developing integrated processes for simultaneous fermentation and product recovery; 3) applying fermentation to upcycle food wastes into valuable products using diverse microorganisms.

Currently Active Projects:
  • Integration of fermentation engineering and synthetic biology for 2,3-butanediol production
  • Conversion of food waste into bioplastic products (PHA) via fermentation

Research Area 3: Techno-Economic Analysis to Evaluate the Economic Feasibility of Novel Processes

One of the most critical questions when developing a new process is: "Is this new process economically viable on a commercial scale?" To answer this, techno-economic analysis is typically required before scaling up for commercialization. Techno-economic analysis involves detailed process modeling, evaluating material inputs and outputs, water usage, and energy balance. These factors are then translated into capital and operating costs, ultimately determining the profitability of the designed process.

Our group is actively working on techno-economic analysis to evaluate the economic feasibility of various processes related to food ingredients and functional material production.

Currently Active Projects:
  • Techno-economic analysis of antioxidant peptides production from corn and sorghum proteins
  • Techno-economic analysis of upgrading biogas through in-situ conversion of carbon dioxide to biomethane
  • Techno-economic analysis of hard carbon production from agricultural wastes
